In this blog, I want to elaborate on the details of 
the article by John Stillwell, _[Galois Theory for Beginners](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2325119)_.

The author provides a rigorous proof of the unsolvability by radicals of the general quintic
equation without most of the classicals tools used to do so.
In fact, only basic facts from groups, rings and fields are needed.
The main tool is the first isomorphism theorem for groups.

As the author states, a proof of the unsolvability by radicals can be provided building upon three ideas:

1. Fields containing  \\(n\\) indeterminates  can be "symmetrized".
2. The Galois group of a radical extension 
is solvable.
3. The symmetric  group  \\(S_n\\) is not solvabl

The author manages to do so in just 6 pages.
However, some ideas are not developed too much,
so I intent to provide some details.
